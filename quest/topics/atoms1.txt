# ---------- Hand-written questions ----------

Isotopes of the same element have the same number of ___ but a different number of ___.
- neutrons; protons
* protons; neutrons
- electrons; protons
- protons; electrons
# Explanation: The number of protons (atomic number) defines the element. Isotopes differ only in their number of neutrons, and so in mass.

Which number tells you which element an atom is?
- The mass number
* The atomic number (number of protons)
- The number of neutrons
- The relative atomic mass
# Explanation: Every atom with 6 protons is carbon, whatever its neutron count. Change the proton number and you change the element.

The mass number of an atom is equal to…
- protons + electrons
- neutrons only
- protons only
* protons + neutrons
# Explanation: Protons and neutrons (nucleons) each have a mass of about 1 u; electrons contribute almost nothing.

Two isotopes of the same element will generally…
- have different chemical properties
- have different numbers of electrons in the neutral atom
- sit in different places on the periodic table
* react in the same way chemically, but differ slightly in mass
# Explanation: Chemistry is governed by electrons. Isotopes have the same number of protons, so the same number of electrons in the neutral atom, and the same chemistry.

The relative atomic mass printed on the periodic table is…
- the mass of the most common isotope
* an abundance-weighted average of the masses of the naturally occurring isotopes
- the simple average of all known isotopes
- the number of protons plus neutrons in one atom
# Explanation: It is a weighted mean, which is why chlorine is 35.45 even though no single chlorine atom has that mass.

Relative atomic mass (Aᵣ) has units of…
- grams
* none: it is a ratio
- g mol⁻¹
- kilograms per atom
# Explanation: Aᵣ compares the average atomic mass with 1/12 of the mass of a ¹²C atom, so the units cancel. Molar mass (g mol⁻¹) has the same number but does have units.

One dalton (1 Da, also written 1 u) is defined as…
- the mass of one proton exactly
- the mass of one hydrogen atom exactly
- 1/16 of the mass of an ¹⁶O atom
* 1/12 of the mass of a ¹²C atom
# Explanation: The unified atomic mass unit and the dalton are the same thing: 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

Hen egg-white lysozyme has a mass of about 14.3 kDa. In daltons, that is…
* 14,300 Da
- 1,430 Da
- 143,000 Da
- 14.3 Da
# Explanation: k means 10³, so 14.3 kDa = 14,300 Da. Protein masses are nearly always quoted in kDa.

Which of these would change if you swapped an atom for a heavier isotope of the same element?
* The mass of the atom
- The number of protons
- The charge on the nucleus
- The number of electrons in the neutral atom
# Explanation: Only the neutron count changes, so only the mass changes. Nuclear charge, and therefore electron count, stay the same.

A sample from a different source has a slightly different relative atomic mass for the same element. The best explanation is…
- its atoms contain a different number of protons
* its isotopic composition is different
- it has been weighed less accurately
- its atoms have lost electrons
# Explanation: Relative atomic mass is a property of a sample, not a constant of nature. Change the mix of isotopes and the weighted average changes.

Deuterium (²H) is an isotope of hydrogen. How many neutrons does a deuterium atom contain?
Elements: H
- 0
* 1
- 2
- 3
# Explanation: Mass number 2 minus 1 proton leaves 1 neutron. Ordinary hydrogen, ¹H, has none.

Compared with an ordinary ¹H atom, a tritium (³H) atom has…
Elements: H
- two extra protons
- two extra electrons
* two extra neutrons
- one extra proton and one extra neutron
# Explanation: All hydrogen isotopes have one proton. Tritium has mass number 3, so it carries two neutrons.

Biochemists run NMR spectra in solvents such as D₂O and CDCl₃. What is the "D"?
Elements: H
- Dysprosium
- A hydrogen ion, H⁺
- Tritium, ³H
* Deuterium, the hydrogen isotope ²H
# Explanation: Deuterium behaves chemically like hydrogen but does not show up in a ¹H NMR spectrum, so the solvent does not swamp the sample.

Helium-3 and helium-4 differ in their number of…
Elements: He
- protons
* neutrons
- electrons
- protons and electrons
# Explanation: Both have 2 protons (that is what makes them helium). ³He has 1 neutron and ⁴He has 2.

The atomic mass unit is defined relative to which isotope?
Elements: C

- ¹H
- ¹⁶O
- ¹³C
# Explanation: 1 u (1 Da) is exactly 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

- It has one more proton
* It has one more neutron
- It has one more electron
- It is radioactive
# Explanation: ¹⁵N is a stable isotope with 8 neutrons rather than 7. Same chemistry, slightly heavier.

Why could Meselson and Stahl separate DNA made with ¹⁵N from DNA made with ¹⁴N?
Elements: N
- ¹⁵N-DNA is radioactive
- ¹⁵N-DNA base-pairs differently
- ¹⁵N-DNA carries more negative charge
* ¹⁵N-DNA is denser

Argon (Aᵣ 39.95) comes before potassium (Aᵣ 39.10) in the periodic table even though it is heavier. Why?
Elements: Ar
- Argon's mass was measured wrongly
- Noble gases are always placed first
- Potassium has more neutrons
* The table is ordered by atomic number, not mass
# Explanation: Argon has 18 protons and potassium 19. Argon's high mass comes from its abundance of the heavy isotope ⁴⁰Ar.

Most argon on Earth is ⁴⁰Ar, made by decay of ⁴⁰K in rocks. Argon from elsewhere in the Solar System has a quite different Aᵣ. This shows that…
Elements: Ar
- argon atoms on other planets have more protons
- Aᵣ values on the periodic table are wrong
* relative atomic mass depends on where the sample came from
- isotopes have different chemistry
# Explanation: Aᵣ is a property of a sample. Different histories give different isotope mixtures.

Why can't ²³⁵U and ²³⁸U be separated by an ordinary chemical reaction?
Elements: U
* Isotopes have the same electron arrangement, so the same chemistry
- Uranium is chemically unreactive
- They have different numbers of protons
- They are both radioactive
# Explanation: Chemistry depends on electrons. Separating isotopes needs a method that exploits their small mass difference instead.
